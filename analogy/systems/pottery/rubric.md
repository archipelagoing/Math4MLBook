## Target and specificity contract

Weighing clay, centering/opening/pulling a wall, caliper profiles, trimming, stage-specific lengths, and finished specimen measurements must explain each record.

This rubric evaluates both the editorial canon and its eventual manuscript use.
Every W1–W5/B topic group needs a source, local action, exact mathematical landing,
boundary, callback, and eventually a checked fresh formal question and chapter
location. Separate the object from its record; distinguish real vector spaces from
physical feasibility. No analogy stands in for a proof or independent exam skill.

## Mandatory rejection tests

1. Wheel centering is equated with subtracting a statistical mean without distinguishing the actions, or a rigid point rotation is presented as the entire hand-driven shaping process.
2. Throwing, trimming, mixing material properties, or firing is declared universally linear/invertible; a dimension inverse is claimed to physically unfire or reconstruct removed clay.
3. Equal component masses or selected caliper summaries are claimed to establish identical history, full shape, wall thickness, material response, quality, or function.
4. Stage-specific shrink percentages with different reference lengths are simply added; hypothetical factors become empirical material/firing recommendations.
5. Profile SVD/PCA is called a literal hand technique, aesthetic verdict, causal mechanism, or evidence of functional/food-safe ware.
6. Any topic is missing, a supplement is falsely sourced to a lecture, a source
   image is described without review, or diagrammatic resemblance replaces mechanism.
7. Dimensions, domains, hypotheses, inverse/rank conditions, singular/zero/repeated
   cases, full/compact/truncated shapes, or exact computations are wrong.
8. Ratings are confused with physical measurements; missing values become zero;
   ordinary SVD is declared a validated missing-data predictor without a model/check.
9. Drafts/calculation checks are called independent review, rendered accessibility,
   real experiments, student readiness, or successful grade outcomes without evidence.

An unresolved rejection blocks a readiness claim regardless of total score.
Correct the claim, narrow the analogy, or explicitly revise the model; do not
compensate for an error with visual polish or unrelated features.

## Review method

Walk the actual route: **PREPARE → CENTER/OPEN/TRIM and SOLVE/COORDINATES/OBSERVE → ORTH/FIT/FINISH/MODES → COMPARE.**

1. Read the canon and build method; compare every topic with its exact lecture
   PDF pages or the exam-topic supplement. Inspect supporting problems when mapped.
2. Apply every rejection test; recompute examples and inspect dimensions, input/output
   spaces, feasible domains, metrics, and ambiguous use of the same word.
3. Check the complete solution, both complement pairs, one unique/invertible case,
   projection/residual, Gram–Schmidt, eigen/SVD distinction, truncation, and PCA.
4. Inspect every action/figure and trace graph callbacks across all five weeks.
   Test the six answered anchors, then develop a fresh bank with equivalent but
   different problems; do not mistake reused examples for independent transfer.
5. Record domain/math review and student performance separately from author checks.
   A vivid metaphor that teaches an incorrect inference must be revised.
6. Assign each score with evidence only after reviewing the current artifact.
   Replace only Latest assessment at later milestones; retain the target/weights
   unless an explicit scope change requires revision.
