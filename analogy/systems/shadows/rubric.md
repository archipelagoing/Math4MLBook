## Target and specificity

Matched marked points, calibrated orthographic screens, hidden depth, independent views, noisy correspondences, frame rotations, and point collections form one connected scene.

Every topic W1.01–W5.07 and B.01–B.02 in the shared register must have a concrete
mechanism, mathematical landing, limitation, callback, and eventually a chapter
location and checked unfamiliar transfer question. Teach the student to solve
formal exam questions, not merely to retell the scene. Matching words without
mechanisms do not count. Keep coordinates, physical quantities, maps, and datasets
stable; identify every model or unit change.

## Mandatory rejection tests

1. Orthographic matched-point coordinates are claimed to reconstruct arbitrary solid objects from silhouettes, occlusion, or unknown correspondences.
2. The 2-by-3 view O, 3-by-3 embedded projector P, and 4-by-4 measurement-space projector Q are conflated.
3. Extra copies of one view are claimed to restore depth; algebraic uniqueness is confused with stability of nearly redundant views.
4. Perspective division or origin translation is silently treated as a linear orthographic map; active and passive rotations are mixed.
5. The strongest singular direction is called intrinsically most important in the object; PCA is claimed to recover unobserved depth.
6. Spaces, dimensions, hypotheses, singular/zero/repeated-value cases, or numerical
   claims are wrong; analogy replaces proof or formal derivation.
7. A lecture topic has no registered route, or a supplemental topic is falsely
   attributed to lecture pages that do not teach it.
8. Domain review, empirical data, rendering accuracy, or successful student learning
   is claimed without evidence. Recommendation ratings are silently substituted for
   physical measurements or missing values are treated as zero.

Any unresolved rejection prevents a readiness claim, regardless of total score.

## Review method

Walk this route: **MARK → ONE → TWO/TURN → FIT/ANGLE → MODES → CLOUD**. Follow every source/topic mapping and the rest of the graph.

Recalculate anchors; check input/output spaces, inverse conditions, full/compact/
truncated shapes, metric and feasibility. Verify physical claims against the linked
domain source. Inspect figures and ordinary formal transfer questions. Use the
matched teaching-sample protocol in the comparison; do not confuse familiarity,
practice effects, enthusiasm, or editorial preference with improved learning.
Replace only Latest assessment at a meaningful milestone; keep comparable weights.
