## The target

Every mathematical concept connects to the actual act of knitting or crochet:
forming a particular stitch, working a repeat, counting old and new stitches,
reading a chart, shaping around a magic ring, measuring a swatch, undoing work,
or finishing an edge. The reader can picture the maker's hands and the yarn,
understand the specific relationship, and then solve the mathematics unaided.

The two fixed projects, operation ledger, and concrete callbacks keep the analogies
connected. Named craft techniques must do explanatory work. Putting “knitting” in
front of a fictional vector or calling any table a “pattern” does not meet the brief.

## Mandatory rejection tests

An entry fails review until corrected if any of these applies:

1. **Replaceability:** Replacing the craft nouns with “widgets” leaves the whole
   explanation unchanged because no actual stitch mechanism is described. A count
   example can still pass when it derives the numbers from explicit stitch actions.
2. **Missing hands:** The entry names no specific stitch, technique, repeat,
   measurable swatch feature, or finishing action and its before/after state.
3. **Decorative technique:** A magic ring is called a vector space, casting off an
   inverse, or crossed needles orthogonality without a valid mathematical mapping.
4. **Lost representation boundary:** Equal counts are claimed to mean equal fabric;
   signed/real solutions become literal instructions; a count-preserving substitution
   is called physically invisible; or an affine update is silently called linear.
5. **Craft inaccuracy:** The instructions miscount stitches, conflate US/UK terms,
   confuse magic ring with magic loop, or conflate knitting bind-off with crochet
   fastening off. Claims about fit, tension, or flatness exceed their evidence.
6. **Broken mathematics:** Dimensions, spaces, conditions, computations, or exceptions
   are wrong; an analogy is offered instead of a derivation or proof.
7. **Disconnected coverage:** A taught concept has no specific registered anchor,
   mathematical landing, and callback into the shared projects.
8. **Unsupported readiness:** A drafted analogy is described as implemented,
   craft-tested, or successful with students without corresponding evidence.

A physical action may serve a modest, honest role. Casting on can introduce an
initial condition and binding off a final constraint check; neither needs to be
falsely equated with an advanced algebraic operator. All mathematical topics still
require explicit connections, but all craft techniques need not represent theorems.

## Entry review card

For every analogy passage, record:

- **Actual action:** Which named stitch/technique? What do the hands do?
- **Before → after:** Old stitches, new stitches, placement, or measured quantities.
- **Observation retained:** Counts, sequence, loops, geometry, or gauge? What is lost?
- **Mathematical claim:** Formula/result, domain, dimensions, and assumptions.
- **Worked evidence:** Check the count and algebra; cite craft and course sources.
- **Callback:** Which earlier/later action card does this deepen?
- **Boundary:** Where would following the metaphor produce an incorrect answer?
- **Transfer:** Fresh ordinary mathematics question, checked answer, and diagnostic feedback.
- **Implementation evidence:** Chapter/figure location and review result; mark pending honestly.

## Review procedure

Read the canon, source materials, and chapter passages. Walk the physical sequence:
cast on or make a magic ring → work individual stitches → count/repeat/chart →
increase/decrease → solve the next row → compare equivalent count totals → fit and
measure swatches → finish/check. Trace the advanced callbacks to those same actions.

Audit every registered course concept, not only the attractive examples. Independently
recalculate stitch totals and algebra. Inspect charts, reading conventions, diagram
labels, and stated yarn/loop mechanics. Check integer versus real feasibility,
rank deficiency, centering, units, and different input/output spaces. Try the
replaceability test. Separate source-backed craft facts from original mathematical
models, physically executed patterns, and learner evidence.

Replace only the latest assessment after a meaningful iteration. A changed target
or weight set starts a new baseline rather than an apparent score improvement.
