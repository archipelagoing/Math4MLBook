# Mathematics, stitch by stitch

<div class="chapter-kicker">Mathematical Foundations of Machine Learning</div>

A row of stitches contains more information than its total. A matrix can retain
some of that information and discard the rest. Learning to say exactly what a
record means—and what a calculation permits you to conclude—is the beginning
of useful mathematics.

This textbook develops the supplied course lectures into explanations you can
study independently. Knitting and crochet provide the recurring examples;
precise definitions, derivations, geometric interpretations, and checked practice
provide the mathematical foundation. You do not need craft experience to begin.

:::{admonition} Start with Lecture 1
:class: key-result

[Vectors and matrices: reading a pattern mathematically](lecture1/index.md)
introduces the language used throughout the course. Follow the explanations,
try the checkpoints, then attempt the mixed practice before consulting solutions.
:::

## A way to study that produces evidence

Read a definition, reproduce a worked example with its steps covered, and solve
a new question unaided. Check not only the final number, but the dimensions,
assumptions, method, and interpretation. Use an incorrect answer to identify the
specific idea to revisit, then attempt a fresh problem.

The goal is strong preparation for the course's exam through understanding and
independent problem solving. Simply recognizing a familiar solution is a different
skill from producing one.

## What is available

The first chapter covers the complete Lecture 1 scope: vectors, linear combinations,
dot products, norms and angles, matrices, special structures, transpose, trace,
and products. Later weeks will develop systems and inverses, vector spaces,
orthogonality and projections, and matrix decomposition. Those chapters are not
yet available in this edition.

Each chapter distinguishes the original course material from added explanations
and examples. [Lecture 1's source notes](lecture1/source-notes.md) give its page-level
coverage and attribution. The diagrams are original schematics of mathematical
records, rather than copied lecture figures or tested craft instructions.
